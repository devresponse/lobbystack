# RealInfoBot in action: use-case scenarios

Real situations real estate agents face, and what RealInfoBot does in each: the evening drive-by, a showing, an open house, the feature sheet at home, buyers in other languages, a busy new listing, a team, an agent who always has a sign up, a rental, a listing presentation and a sale. Each scenario ends with the plan that usually fits.

## Scenario: a buyer drives past the sign at 8 pm

A couple slows down at the sign at 8 pm. They like the house and want the price, but they won't call an agent that late, and they won't leave a voicemail.

With RealInfoBot, they scan the QR code on the sign. A chat about that home opens on their phone, and they ask the price, whether the basement is finished and when they could see it. The assistant answers from the agent's facts and takes their name and number for a showing.

The agent reads the inquiry at breakfast, with a summary and the transcript, and calls them first. Without RealInfoBot, the agent would never have known they existed.

Best fit: the 1-listing pack, CA$99 for 180 days, for a single listing.

## Scenario: the agent is at a showing when the sign gets a call

The agent is walking another family through a home. The phone buzzes: a call from someone parked at another listing's sign. Answering means interrupting the showing; not answering means voicemail, and most buyers hang up without leaving one.

With RealInfoBot, the call goes to the listing's RealInfoBot number, and the assistant answers it out loud, answers the caller's questions and takes their details. If the caller wants the agent right away, it can put them through to the agent's phone, if the agent set a transfer number, or take a message.

The agent finishes the showing, sees "asked about possession date, wants a call back today" with the transcript, and calls back knowing what to say.

Best fit: any listing pack, since phone calls need a RealInfoBot number.

Also asked as: busy at a showing, can't answer the phone, missed calls, while driving, with a client, overflow calls.

## Scenario: an open house with twenty groups

Twenty groups come through in two hours. The agent can talk to three at a time. Some visitors are too shy to ask questions in front of others.

With RealInfoBot, the agent puts a card with the listing's QR code on the front door and the sign-in table, each with its own QR link, such as "Door card" and "Sign-in table". Visitors scan and ask on the way back to the car. A rule such as "If someone asks about the open house, mention it's this Saturday from 2 to 4" keeps the assistant up to date beforehand.

Afterwards, the agent opens the inbox and the Questions list, follows up with the serious buyers first, answers new questions once, and sees which card buyers scanned.

Best fit: the listing's pack, with a QR link for each card.

Also asked as: open house, sign-in sheet, open house leads, shy buyers, weekend open house, visitors, door card.

## Scenario: the feature sheet goes home with a buyer

A buyer takes the feature sheet home. At 10 pm, at the kitchen table, they wonder about the condo fees and whether pets are allowed. Calling an agent at that hour feels wrong.

With RealInfoBot, the QR code printed on the feature sheet opens the listing's chat. The buyer asks, gets the answer from the agent's facts, and leaves an email address for more details. If the agent hasn't covered pets yet, the assistant says it doesn't have that detail, takes a message, and adds the question to the Questions list.

The next morning, the agent answers the buyer and adds the pet policy once, so every buyer after gets it.

Best fit: any plan; the free plan's 5 chats a month are enough to try it, and a pack for a listing that's on the market.

Also asked as: feature sheet, flyer, brochure, kitchen-table questions, late-night questions, condo fees, pet policy, print marketing.

## Scenario: buyers who speak another language

A listing is in a neighbourhood where many buyers are more comfortable in Punjabi, Chinese or Spanish, or are shopping for a parent who is.

With RealInfoBot, a buyer scans the sign and types in Punjabi, or calls and speaks Spanish. The assistant answers in their language, from the facts the agent wrote once in English, and never adds anything the agent didn't give it. The buyer asks fuller questions in their first language and leaves their name.

The agent reads the summary, written in the agent's language, and the transcript in the morning, and calls back.

Why it matters: a wider pool of buyers for the same listing, without hiring help.

Best fit: any plan. Every language comes with every listing, with nothing to switch on.

Also asked as: multilingual buyers, immigrant buyers, newcomers, Punjabi, Chinese, Mandarin, Spanish, French, Hindi, Korean, language barrier.

## Scenario: a new listing gets a rush of interest

A new listing goes up on a Friday and the weekend is busy: calls, scans and the same questions again and again.

With RealInfoBot, every caller and every chat gets an answer at the same time: each conversation is its own, so nobody waits for another buyer to finish. The assistant answers the price, taxes and open house questions from the agent's facts, and takes the showing requests.

On Monday, the agent has a list of conversations with summaries, sorted by what each buyer wanted, and calls the showing requests first. If the listing is busier than the pack's minutes allow, a top-up of 100 extra minutes costs CA$39.

Best fit: a listing pack; the 3-listing pack's 750 minutes give room for busy listings.

Also asked as: busy listing, hot listing, lots of calls, many calls at once, simultaneous calls, new listing launch, coming soon.

## Scenario: a team with several listings and several people

A team has four listings. A partner covers showings, an assistant returns calls and the team lead wants to see what's coming in. Today that means forwarded voicemails, screenshots of texts, and leads that fall between people.

With RealInfoBot, each listing has its own assistant, page and QR codes, and every inquiry lands in one shared inbox. Each person signs in with their own login, as an admin or a viewer, and picks their own alerts. Rules apply to every listing, so every sign handles buyers the same way. Plans don't charge per team member.

The team's assistant follows up on a callback without being forwarded anything, and the team lead reads the calls and contacts.

Best fit: the 6-listing pack, CA$449 for up to 6 listings at once for 365 days. For a whole brokerage, email support@realinfobot.com.

Also asked as: team example, real estate team, partners, assistant, shared inbox, several agents, team lead, brokerage example.

## Scenario: an agent who always has a sign up

An agent takes listings all year. They want the lowest price per listing, no subscription billing them in slow months, and the buyers who called about one home on hand when the next one comes up.

With RealInfoBot's 6-listing pack, CA$449 for 365 days, up to 6 listings answer at once. When a home sells, the agent pauses its assistant and adds the next listing in its place, and points the same sign rider's QR code at it. The pack's minutes and chats are shared by all its listings, and a top-up adds minutes if a busy season needs more.

Everyone who called, texted or chatted stays in the contacts, with their transcripts, so a buyer who wasn't right for one home is on hand for the next.

Best fit: the 6-listing pack works out to about CA$75 a listing, against CA$99 for a single one.

Also asked as: high-volume agent, many listings a year, top producer, lowest price per listing, bulk, yearly, always listing.

## Scenario: a landlord or property manager with a rental

A landlord has a home for rent. The phone rings all week with the same questions: "Is it still available?", "Are pets allowed?", "Is parking included?", "When can I see it?"

With RealInfoBot, the "For rent" sign carries the listing's QR code, and with a pack, its RealInfoBot number. The assistant answers those questions from the landlord's facts, any hour, and takes the details of people who want a viewing, or books viewings into open times when booking is turned on.

The landlord reads the inquiries with their summaries and calls back the people worth meeting.

Best fit: the 1-listing pack for one rental, or the 3-listing or 6-listing pack for several units at once.

Also asked as: rental, for rent, for lease, landlord, property manager, tenants, rental inquiries, apartment, leasing.

## Scenario: winning over a seller at the listing presentation

An agent is competing for a listing. The seller wants to know how the agent will make sure every interested buyer is heard.

With RealInfoBot, the agent can show it working: let the seller scan a sign or chat with an assistant on the spot, and explain that their sign will answer buyers day and night, in the buyer's language, from facts the seller approves, with every question reaching the agent. During the listing, the agent can share what buyers ask about most and how many people scanned the sign.

To try it before the presentation, the sample listing on https://realinfobot.com/ca can be scanned or called, and the free plan lets the agent set up a listing of their own.

Best fit: the 1-listing pack for that listing, or the 3-listing pack if the agent lists a few homes a year.

Also asked as: listing presentation, listing appointment, win the listing, seller meeting, pitch to sellers, stand out, differentiate.

## Scenario: the home sells before the pack ends

The home sells in three weeks, and the agent's pack has months left.

With RealInfoBot, nothing is wasted and nothing needs cancelling. The agent pauses or archives the sold home's assistant and adds their next listing in its place, for as long as the pack lasts. The sign rider's QR code can point to the new listing, so a printed code keeps working. The calls, chats and contacts from the sold home are kept.

If there's no next listing yet, the pack simply ends on its date. Nothing renews.

Also asked as: sold early, listing sold, what if it sells fast, unused time, cancel, transfer to another listing, swap listings.
