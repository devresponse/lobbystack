# Sell prepaid minute packs

A business on a cloud deployment can pay once for a pack of voice minutes that lasts a set number of days, instead of a monthly plan: 500 minutes for 180 days, for example. The deployment's billing provider, Stripe or Polar, only takes the payment. LobbyStack keeps the minutes, the end date and what each call drew from them, the same way on both providers and in both of Stripe's selling modes. Packs are an additional option: a business that never buys one keeps its plan, its allowances, its prices and its bills exactly as before. Built on `releases/r2` as migration `0148`, with `0149` from its review, from the design of 2026-11-09 and the maintainer's [decisions](../project/decisions.md#choose-what-to-build).

## Know what a pack is

- **One payment, no subscription.** No renewal and no overage bill. When the minutes run out, calls stop as they do on Free, until the business buys another pack.
- **A length in days, fixed at the payment.** A pack ends at its payment time plus its days, counted as 24-hour days, so no month length or clock change shortens it and a later change to the catalog never moves it.
- **No carry-over.** Each pack ends on its own date. A business can hold several, and calls draw from the one that ends first, so buying early loses only minutes the business couldn't have used in time. Unused minutes are gone at the pack's end, even while another pack runs.
- **Per business.** A pack is never shared with the owner's other businesses.

## Know the packs on sale

Each brand's `billing.json` has a `packs` section beside its plans. Each size and length is a pack of its own, with its own code, its names in English, French, Spanish and Serbian, its minutes, its days and its one-time price in the file's currency, and its own Stripe price or Polar product. AlloPlace sells nine:

| Pack | 90 days | 180 days | 365 days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 250 minutes | CA$55 | CA$59 | CA$65 |
| 500 minutes | CA$95 | CA$99 | CA$109 |
| 1,000 minutes | CA$179 | CA$189 | CA$199 |

These are starting prices for the maintainer to set before live payments, not decisions: change `priceCents` in AlloPlace's file, then run `pnpm billing apply`. Stripe never changes a price, so for a pack already linked that run makes a new Stripe price and needs `--allow-archive` and `--allow-replace` ([Stripe billing](../providers/stripe.md#know-what-apply-makes-for-packs)); Polar puts the new price on the pack's own product.

RealInfoBot sells three listing packs and no plan, from realinfobot.com's pricing table (decided on 2026-11-10). A listing is a receptionist: a pack's listings are how many receptionists can be active at once while it lasts, not credits used up. Each listing brings 250 minutes for the pack's whole length, and the monthly allowances and knowledge grow with the listings. Each pack includes one phone number, the most a pack can:

| Pack | Price | Listings (receptionists) | Minutes | Days | Alert text segments, transfers and website chats a month | Knowledge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `listing_1_180` | CA$99 | 1 | 250 | 180 | 50, 20 and 50 | 20 MB |
| `listings_3_365` | CA$249 | 3 | 750 | 365 | 150, 60 and 150 | 60 MB |
| `listings_6_365` | CA$449 | 6 | 1,500 | 365 | 300, 120 and 300 | 120 MB |

Its file keeps Starter and Pro unsold, since every file holds the four standard plans, and Enterprise, which its plan screens don't show. Its plan step and the **Upgrade plan** dialog show the Free card and a card for each pack, as on realinfobot.com's pricing table: the pack's name, its price, paid once, its words and its own button, with the 3 listings pack marked **Best value** and each card's description giving its price per listing and the saving over single listings ([onboarding](onboarding.md#word-the-plan-step-from-billingjson)). The cards show once an apply puts the packs on sale, and the plan step's packs' box no longer shows. **Settings > Plan** picks a pack by its minutes, 250, 750 or 1,500, each with its only length, and doesn't name the listings, which the pack's name does. The pricing table's "call or text" and its 30-day refund promise aren't in the app's words: the receptionist never answers texts, and a refund is made by hand in the provider's dashboard.

A pack's monthly allowances and limits are Starter's unless the pack sets its own: 50 alert text segments, 20 call transfers and 50 website chats a month, one phone number and one receptionist. AlloPlace's packs set 20 MB of knowledge where Starter has 5 MB, and RealInfoBot's set every allowance and limit themselves. A pack's `days` runs from 1 to 1,095; a pack with no `days` never ends, a setting the brands don't use. [`brands/README.md`](../../brands/README.md#prepaid-packs) describes the section.

## Know what a business gets

While a business that would otherwise be on Free holds a live pack, the plan in force is **prepaid**, a sixth tier beside self-hosted, Free, Starter, Pro and Enterprise. It is never an account's own plan nor a plan on sale: `billing_accounts` keeps what the provider last reported, and the code works prepaid out from `billing_packs` each time. A pack paid while a managed, yearly or past-due plan is in force waits unused beside it, with no reminders, and its end date still comes.

- **Voice minutes** are the live packs' balance, not a monthly allowance. A pack's minutes last its whole length.
- **Alert texts, transfers and website chats** are counted per UTC month against the pack's monthly allowances, as on a plan, and refused past them, as on Free.
- **Limits** are the most any live pack gives: its knowledge storage, its number and its receptionists. Content is kept as on a paying plan.
- **A phone number.** `app.business_included_numbers` returns the live pack's number first, so a pack business can claim one, which Free can't.
- **Nothing goes to the provider.** Calls on a pack are recorded with the plan `prepaid` and sync status `skipped`, so the billing sweep never sends them, and no meter sees them.

## Know how calls use the minutes

- **A call reserves minutes before it's answered,** as on Free: 5-minute slices for a phone call, the whole 30 minutes for a website call. On a pack the reservation draws on the packs, earliest end first, under a lock per business rather than per month, since a balance spans months. `billing_pack_draws` records which pack paid for each call.
- **At zero,** a phone call gets a busy signal (SIP 486) and a website call shows the limit, as on Free.
- **When a call ends,** what it didn't use goes back to the pack it last drew from. A call that crossed two packs keeps what it took from each.
- **A frozen pack** (a dispute is open) and a pack whose end has come aren't drawn from.

## Buy a pack

Owners and admins buy a pack under **Prepaid minutes** in **Settings > Plan**, or under **Prefer prepaid minutes?** below the plans in onboarding's plan step. They pick its **Minutes** and how long it **Lasts**, see its price "paid once", and click **Buy pack**, which Settings > Plan calls **Buy more** while the business holds a pack. Where the brand's `billing.json` gives packs cards of their own, as RealInfoBot's does, each pack on sale is a card beside the plans' on the plan step, in place of **Prefer prepaid minutes?**, and in the **Upgrade plan** dialog, with its price "paid once" and a button of its own that buys it the same way ([onboarding](onboarding.md#word-the-plan-step-from-billingjson)). Settings > Plan also lists the packs held, each with its minutes left out of its total and its end date, and "On hold while a payment dispute is open" while a dispute holds it. Members who don't manage billing see only the packs held, and the section doesn't show on a self-hosted deployment or on Enterprise, managed or not.

The button works for a role of `business_admin` or higher, on a deployment whose provider has what a pack checkout needs (`STRIPE_API_KEY` and `STRIPE_ACCOUNT_ID` on Stripe, `POLAR_ACCESS_TOKEN` and `POLAR_ORGANIZATION_ID` on Polar), for a pack a link has put on sale. Otherwise it's greyed out, with the refusal under it or "Packs can't be bought here right now.". A purchase that would end a monthly Starter or Pro plan first asks **End your Starter plan?** or **End your Pro plan?**: the plan ends at once, the rest of its month isn't refunded and its usage so far is still billed, a promo code is lost for good, Pro's receptionists but one pause, and the pack starts. **End plan and buy** goes on.

The admin records a pack checkout with the pack's terms as the business saw them (its minutes, days, monthly allowances and limits) and the subscription live on the account then, which its payment replaces ([below](#switch-between-a-pack-and-a-subscription)), and queues `billing.createPackCheckout`. The payment adds the pack with those terms, so a catalog apply while the page is open, or before a bank debit settles, never gives less than was paid for; a checkout from before `0149` keeps none, and its payment reads the catalog. The worker asks the provider for a one-time payment page:

- **Stripe directly:** a Checkout Session in payment mode on the business's own customer, with Stripe Tax, the billing address and tax ID, and an invoice.
- **Stripe as the merchant of record** (both brands): the same session with Managed Payments on, and without the tax parameters Stripe's guide says to leave out.
- **Polar:** a checkout for the pack's one-time product.

The screen goes to the payment page once it's ready. When the provider won't make the business's customer with the person's email, a form asks for a billing email and starts again with it, as a plan checkout does. A checkout that waits more than 15 minutes for its job expires, and the billing sweep's `pack_checkout` branch queues the job again for one it finds unfinished.

After paying, the provider sends the person back with `?pack=success&packCheckoutId=<id>` to **Settings > Plan**, or to onboarding's plan step while onboarding isn't complete. The page waits up to 90 seconds for the provider's payment event, then says "Your pack was added.", that the payment didn't go through, or that it's processing and the pack shows once it arrives. Onboarding reads billing again once the pack is paid and moves on to the number step, or past it when the plan in force includes no number, as **Continue** does. A payment whose pack ended as it arrived says so instead: a plan the business bought started first, so the pack wasn't added and the payment will be refunded, or the payment was refunded or charged back before it arrived. Onboarding then moves on with the plan in force, or shows the plans again after a refund. A payment made on a page that a plan's start closed, which Polar's page and Stripe's before its close still take, is waited on the same way: the page says the pack gave way once the payment arrives, or when the 90 seconds run out, since its pack ends as it arrives either way. The checkout names the pack its payment added (`billing_pack_checkouts.pack_id`), which is how the page tells them apart. A Stripe payment that settles later, such as a bank debit, adds the pack when `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` arrives. Leaving the payment page unpaid returns to the plan page without the marker.

A business can't buy a pack when the checkout refuses it, in this order, after one check that comes before all of them: on a deployment whose provider isn't set up for packs, the dashboard answers `pack_not_on_sale` and the API its 409, whatever else holds. The plan screens check the first five before the click: the first two hide the packs on sale, and the next three show under the greyed-out button. The dashboard's route answers a refusal with a 409 and its code, which the screen says in the person's language, and the API with a 409 `conflict` and the reason:

| Refusal | Why |
| --- | --- |
| `managed_plan` | An operator gave it a managed plan (Enterprise, billed directly) |
| `pack_not_sold_on_plan` | The deployment is self-hosted, or the business is on Enterprise |
| `pack_yearly_plan` | It's on a yearly plan: set it to end at renewal and buy a pack then (decision 6a) |
| `pack_past_due` | A plan payment is past due: settle it first (decision 6b) |
| `plan_change_payment_waiting` | An upgrade of its plan still waits for its payment |
| `pack_not_on_sale` | The pack is retired, back in draft after new terms, or not linked to the provider yet |
| `plan_change_pending` | A plan checkout, subscription change or other pack checkout of the business, made in the last 15 minutes, is still pending or processing: one purchase at a time, across packs and plans. A payment page already made holds nothing |

The same person asking again for the same pack and billing email while their checkout is pending or processing gets that checkout back.

## Switch between a pack and a subscription

A business can move either way, and minutes or allowances left at the switch are gone (decision 6). Each step acts only when a pack is involved.

- **Pack to subscription.** A pack business buys Starter or Pro through the plan checkout, which works for it as for Free, and whose dialog warns that starting a plan ends the packs and the minutes left on them. A promo code's plan can be bought from a pack too, under **Have a promo code?**, which gives the same warning. The warning counts every pack held, frozen ones too, so a business on Free whose only pack a dispute froze sees it as well; a plan changed in place ends no pack and shows none. When the subscription goes live, `billing.reconcile` ends the business's live and frozen packs as `replaced`, records the minutes each still held with a `billing.pack_replaced` audit row, takes back a number release the last pack's end set, and starts the subscription's allowances from that moment, in the same transaction and under the same lock as the account. It also closes the business's pack checkouts still open: one still being made fails, and a payment page already made expires, which `billing.closePackCheckout` then closes at the provider. Stripe expires the Checkout Session; Polar has no such call, so its page stays payable until its own expiry. The page waiting on such a checkout says a plan started for the business, so this pack's checkout closed, and `lobbystack billing buy-pack --wait` says so without suggesting another checkout: a pack bought now would end a monthly plan, and a yearly one has to end first. A payment made on the page anyway is the next bullet's case, and the business's deletion or cancellation waits until the page's own expiry.
- **Subscription to pack.** A subscriber on a monthly Starter or Pro plan buys a pack. When its payment arrives, one transaction adds the pack, marks the subscription as replaced (`billing_accounts.replaced_subscription_id`) so LobbyStack treats it as ended at once, starts the pack's monthly allowances from that moment, and queues `billing.endPlanForPack`. That job waits up to 32 minutes after the switch, the longest call and two minutes, for calls that began on the plan to end, unless the plan would renew first. A call still running counts whether or not it reserved minutes, since Starter or Pro without a spending cap records a call only when it ends; such a call is recorded under the plan the pack replaced, just before the switch, goes to the provider with that plan's usage, and draws nothing from the pack. The job then sends the plan's usage still pending and ends the subscription at the provider with nothing credited. On Stripe it ends at once: a plan that carries the overage prices, as with Stripe as the merchant of record, ends with `invoice_now`, so its last invoice bills the usage on its meters; on Stripe directly the plan ends alone, and its overage subscription ends with its month, whose invoice on the 1st bills the usage. On Polar it's set to cancel at its period's end, which the job moves to 5 minutes ahead, so the final order bills the usage. The job looks again every minute until the provider shows the plan ended, for up to an hour past the end, then tells the operator; the daily expiry job queues it again while the account still shows the plan live.
- **When the payment finds another plan.** A pack paid while a yearly, past-due or managed plan is in force, which the checkout refuses, leaves that plan alone, with an `ops.billing.pack_beside_subscription` warning in the logs. A subscription that started after the pack's purchase began, such as one bought in another tab while the pack's payment page was open, is the business's latest choice, so the pack ends at once as `replaced` and the subscription stays. The checkout says which: since `0149` it keeps the subscription live when the business asked for it (`billing_pack_checkouts.subscription_id`), so a live subscription it doesn't name, a new one or one that wasn't live then, such as an unpaid plan paid since, came later, whatever order the subscription's events arrived in. A payment that names no checkout, or a checkout from before `0149`, goes by the first event LobbyStack stored about the subscription, against when the purchase began: on Stripe when the Checkout Session was made, on Polar when LobbyStack recorded the pack checkout. When the worker applies the subscription's start after the pack's payment, as events can arrive out of order, the start ends the pack then, after the page may have said it was added, and reports it the same way if the subscription went live before the payment: from the provider's own start (Stripe's `start_date` or `created`, Polar's `started_at` or `created_at`) for a subscription new to the account, and otherwise from the time of the event that made it live, as the provider sent it, or when LobbyStack read the subscription back live if the event it applied was sent before that. A pack in force when the plan started ends unreported, as the first bullet says, but one paid before the plan went live whose payment arrives after the start is reported, since that pack was never in force. LobbyStack refunds nothing (decision 5), so the worker reports the payment for the operator to refund in the provider's dashboard, as the maintainer chose on 2026-11-09: an `ops.billing.pack_gave_way` warning, PostHog event and `billing.pack_gave_way` audit row, each naming the payment. The PostHog event, like every business's product event, isn't recorded for a business that turned off **Share telemetry data**; the warning line and the audit row always are, so alert on one of them for every payment. The refund's event then lands on the ended pack.
- **Fresh allowances only for a switch** (decision 6c). `billing_accounts.allowances_since` marks where the month's allowances start counting; only a switch with a pack writes it, so a business that moves from Free to Starter counts its whole month as before. Pack usage never counts against another plan, Free or a subscription, since the pack paid for it, and a call stays in the part of the month it started in: a call that began on Free keeps Free's allowance when a frozen pack comes back or another pack is bought while it runs.

Worth knowing: a promo-code subscriber who switches to a pack loses the code for good, since a code is redeemed once per business, and from the pack's payment on, before the provider has ended the plan, the code finds nothing, as it does once the plan has ended (migration `0149`); moving from Pro to a pack pauses all but one receptionist, as a downgrade does; and a business moving from a pack to Starter drops from 20 MB to 5 MB of knowledge: nothing is deleted, but uploads stop until it's under 5 MB.

## Know when a pack ends

The worker's `billing.expirePacks` runs once a day for every business that isn't canceled, and returns at once for one that never bought a pack. It also runs 35 minutes after a call's reservation takes the live balance to a fifth or to nothing, and at once when a refund or a dispute ends a pack. One run:

1. **Ends each pack whose end has come,** live or frozen, as `expired`, keeping the minutes it still held for the accounts.
2. **Settles the business's last pack.** When no live pack is left, nor a frozen one with time left, receptionists past what the plan includes pause, and on a plan without a number the number stops answering at once: calls are turned away and texts get no reply, as on a canceled account. The number goes back to Twilio 30 days after the last pack ended, unless a new pack, a subscription or an operator's managed plan comes first, which clears the release and the number answers again. A pack a subscription replaced is never settled here, and neither is one that ended as it arrived, refunded first or given way to a plan, since it switched nothing.
3. **Emails the owners** in their language, English, French, Spanish or Serbian, while a pack is the plan in force: when a fifth of the live balance is left, when none is, and 30 and 7 days before a pack ends. Packs waiting beside a managed, yearly or past-due plan get none, since what the emails say about calls and the number doesn't hold then; the reminders still due come once a pack is the plan again. A reminder that would fall before the purchase is skipped, and `billing_packs.notices` keeps each pack's reminders to once. The balance reminders wait 35 minutes, past the longest call, so they read what the call used rather than what it reserved, and every run, the daily one and a refund's too, reads the balance without what calls still running drew up front, so the owners never hear none is left while a call holds the last minutes. A hold counts as used once it is 35 minutes old, so a call that never settled can't hide the balance. A canceled business gets none.
4. **Queues `billing.endPlanForPack` again** for a subscription a pack replaced, while the account still shows it live.

## Know what refunds and disputes do

LobbyStack never refunds on its own and has no refund command (decision 5). The operator refunds in the Stripe or Polar dashboard, and whoever refunds, the provider's event changes the pack:

- **A partial refund** takes its share of the minutes bought off the pack: a refund of a third of the price removes a third of them. Minutes calls already used stay used, so a pack that used more than the refund leaves it has none left. The provider reports the whole amount refunded so far, so a repeated or late event never takes off more.
- **A refund of the whole payment** ends the pack as `refunded` when the refund's event arrives, so a number the end of the last pack sets to go back to Twilio stays 30 days from then.
- **A refund or lost dispute before the payment.** Stripe can deliver a refund or a dispute's close before the payment's own event, and a Polar order carries its refunds, so whichever of its events comes first adds the pack with them. A payment wholly refunded, or whose dispute was lost, adds the pack as `refunded` and switches nothing: the plan in force, its allowances and a number set to go stay as they were, and no `billing.pack_purchased` event is recorded.
- **A dispute opened** freezes the pack: calls can't draw on it until the dispute closes. Disputes come from Stripe only, since Polar sends no dispute event. A Stripe dispute names only the payment, so once the deployment has linked a pack's price, the webhook reads the payment intent back for its business with the admin's `STRIPE_API_KEY`, which needs read on PaymentIntents; a dispute on a plan's payment stays unattributed, as before. A key without that permission leaves a pack's dispute unattributed too, with a warning: the delivery is answered 200, Stripe doesn't send it again, and the pack is never frozen, even once the key is fixed.
- **A dispute won**, or an inquiry closed without a chargeback, makes the pack live again, or expired if its end passed meanwhile. **A dispute lost** ends it as `refunded`. A dispute lost on a pack that had already ended, by its date or a subscription, keeps why it ended and records the whole payment as refunded, with nothing of it left unused.
- **Out of order.** Stripe can deliver a dispute's close before its opening. A close applied first leaves the pack as it was, or ends it when lost, and the later opening then freezes nothing. Each dispute is matched by its own id, so a chargeback after a closed inquiry on the same payment still freezes the pack.

A pack is matched by its Stripe PaymentIntent or Polar order, not by an invoice, since a pack sold with Stripe as the merchant of record has none.

## See packs in the API and the CLI

- `GET /v1/billing` lists the business's packs under `packs`: the live and frozen ones, the one that ends first first, then up to 20 that ended, the newest first, each with the seconds it holds after refunds, those used and those left, its payment, end date and status. `plan` reads `prepaid` on the prepaid tier, and a subscription a pack replaced reads as ended.
- `POST /v1/billing/packs/checkout` with the pack's code (`pack`, and `billing_email` when needed) answers 202 with a checkout, which `GET /v1/billing/packs/checkout/{checkout_id}` follows until it's `ready`, with `checkout_url` to send to whoever pays, then `paid`, when `pack_status` says what became of the pack: `active` or `frozen` while the business has it, `expired` once its days ran out, `replaced` once a plan the business bought ended it, at once when the plan came before the payment, and `refunded` when its payment went back. Both need a key with `billing:manage`. A deployment whose provider isn't set up for packs answers 409, and a platform key's checkout is made out to the business's owner.
- `pnpm lobbystack billing buy-pack <pack>` does the same, with `--wait` for the page and `--billing-email`, and says what became of a pack already paid, or, for a checkout a plan's start closed, that the plan came first, without suggesting another checkout; `pnpm lobbystack billing pack-status <checkout_id>` checks it. `pnpm lobbystack billing status` shows the packs after the plan, and with an operator's platform key `pnpm lobbystack billing catalog` lists the deployment's packs with their status, live price and link.
- `pnpm lobbystack onboard` accepts a live pack as a plan, and onboarding takes the number limit from the plan in force.
- The dashboard reads `packOffers`, `packs` and `packCheckout` from `GET /api/billing`, and starts and follows a checkout through `POST` and `GET /api/billing/packs/checkout`, whose `packStatus`, `synced` and `closedForPlan` say what became of it. The `POST` answers 409 `pack_not_on_sale` on a deployment whose provider isn't set up for packs, as the API does.

## Set it up on a deployment

1. **Deploy the admin and the worker first.** The apply sends the file through the instance's API, and an instance built before migration `0148` refuses a file with `packs`.
2. **Apply the catalog** once after the deploy: `pnpm billing apply --brand <brand> --stripe live` (or `--polar ...`). The packs section changes the file's hash, so the apply is needed even where no plan changed; every plan's terms hash stays as it was. Until then `pnpm billing verify` reports drift, while the daily catalog check only warns that the code was built with another file (`code_hash`). On Stripe the apply makes a product and a one-time price per pack, with the brands' SaaS tax code, and, since the file has packs, adds three events to the webhook endpoint: `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, `checkout.session.async_payment_failed` and `charge.dispute.closed`. A file without packs leaves the endpoint as it was before packs, an apply never takes the events off, and the webhook ignores them until a link has recorded a pack's price. On Polar it makes a private one-time product per pack.
3. **Check it:** `pnpm billing verify` should report no drift, and the daily catalog check covers every pack.
4. **On Stripe, give the admin's key read on PaymentIntents,** beside its portal and customer permissions, so a dispute of a pack's payment finds its business ([Stripe billing](../providers/stripe.md#know-the-keys)). A restricted key made for an earlier release lacks it.
5. **Polar needs no new token scope:** refunds stay in Polar's dashboard, and ending a plan a pack replaced uses the worker's `subscriptions:write`, as plan changes do.

Rolling back to a release before `0148` leaves the migration in place, and the older code reads none of its tables: a business on a pack falls back to the plan its account records, and the older worker runs none of the pack jobs, so no pack ends, reminds or ends a plan it replaced. The older release also trips over the packs: its `pnpm billing apply` refuses to run while the pack products and prices are live, unless `--allow-archive` archives them, and its daily catalog check fails on the `packs` of the profile last applied. Applying the file without `packs` before rolling back avoids both: it retires the packs and archives their provider objects, and packs already bought keep their minutes. That run needs `--allow-archive`, and `--allow-undeployed` too, since the running code was built with the file that has them. Otherwise, run the older release's `pnpm billing apply --allow-archive` once it's back: it archives the pack objects and stores its own file, though the pack rows stay as they were until a later release applies packs again.

## Know what isn't built or tried

- **Tried only in code.** The flows above have run against PostgreSQL with Stripe and Polar stood in for, not against a Stripe sandbox, a Polar sandbox or a real call. [Open items](../project/open-items.md#test-these) lists the runs still to make, among them the two the design asked for before live payments: a Stripe test-clock run that shows whether ending a merchant of record subscription with `invoice_now` bills the meters' usage, which the sandbox scenarios' `pack-plan-end` makes ([Stripe billing](../providers/stripe.md#run-the-sandbox-scenarios)), and a Polar sandbox run of the period moved ahead.
- **Known gaps.** LobbyStack doesn't read the expiry of a Polar checkout and takes its page to last an hour (`PACK_CHECKOUT_PAGE_MS`): past that the checkout reads as expired, though a payment that still lands adds the pack. Nor can it close a Polar page when a subscription starts, so a pack paid on it gives way to the plan and its payment is reported for a refund by hand; the business's deletion and cancellation wait until that page's own expiry. A pack paid on a page opened before an operator gave the business a managed plan is kept, and waits unused while the managed plan lasts. When a pack's payment event arrives after its disputes, they are judged by payment, not one by one, so an inquiry closed and a chargeback opened, both before that late event, leave the pack live. No API call lists the packs on sale to a business's key: `lobbystack billing buy-pack` takes the code from the brand's `billing.json`, such as `pack_500_180`. A problem older than packs reaches them too: a plan's checkout needs `POLAR_ACCESS_TOKEN` and `POLAR_ORGANIZATION_ID` even on Stripe, so on a Stripe deployment without them neither the plan screens nor the API offer one, and a pack business there can't move to a subscription ([billing](../billing/README.md#know-the-known-problems)).
- **Left out of the first version:** automatic top-ups; holding a pack and a subscription at once; forwarding a call at zero minutes; a refund command; and an operator command that moves one business's end date.
