# Give a business several receptionists

Since fork PR #129, a business can have more than one receptionist: one for each location, team or language, each with its own name, greeting, language, transfer number, booking, knowledge and page, answering the phone numbers, website widgets and QR signs the owner gives it. Every business has one **default** receptionist, made with the business and named **Front desk** in its language. It answers anything that names no receptionist and stands in for one that is paused or archived, so a number, a widget or a printed sign always reaches someone. A business with one receptionist sees the dashboard as before: the dropdowns, filters and columns below only show once it has a second. The maintainer's nine answers to the design's questions are under [decisions](../project/decisions.md#choose-what-to-build), and what is left for later is on the [roadmap](../roadmap/receptionists-next.md). Checked against the code of fork PR #129, with fork PR #132's fixes from its review: ids in any letter case, the number on a receptionist's page, the receptionist a text names, QR links to an unpublished page, receptionists on the plan screens and website rule paths typed with accents; fork PR #133's: what the page offers when the plan is full, the default receptionist's name when the language changes, and a note that **Available to** keeps nothing secret; fork PR #134's: a plan that has had subscribers can't include fewer receptionists; and fork PR #135's, for new deployments only: every business gets its default receptionist from the database, and every call, chat and follow-up names the receptionist that answered it (migration `0143`). Four fixes from the last review came straight to the branch on 2026-11-06: what **Make default** does to a receptionist's knowledge, a snapshot refresh when the business page is published or unpublished, website rules applied on a restore, and no language of its own for the default receptionist.

## Know what each receptionist has

Each receptionist has its own:

- **name** (up to 80 characters) and **What it helps with**, one line of up to 160 characters, such as "Bookings at our West 4th clinic", which visitors see when they choose who to talk to and the other receptionists use to send people there;
- **page address**, `/profile/<slug>/<handle>`, made from its name unless the owner types one; the default receptionist answers the business page, `/profile/<slug>`, itself;
- **language** its calls open in, or the business's language when it has none; the default receptionist always speaks the business's language;
- **greeting, transfer number and booking**, on **AI settings**, and the instructions for calls, texts and chats that a new receptionist copies from another;
- **knowledge**: **Every article**, as the default receptionist has, or **Articles for everyone, and the ones chosen for it** ([give each receptionist its knowledge](#give-each-receptionist-its-knowledge));
- **place on the business page**: listed or not, and its place in the order;
- **status**: active, paused or archived.

The business keeps one copy of everything else, which every receptionist shares: the business summary that every receptionist's prompts start from, **Tell callers about AI and recording**, the business's language and time zone, opening hours, services, rules, the appointment change rules, contacts, the knowledge library, the plan and its usage. Opening hours stay the business's: hours for each receptionist are on the [roadmap](../roadmap/receptionists-next.md).

## Add and manage receptionists

**Agent > Receptionists** (`/agent/receptionists`), between **AI settings** and **Knowledge** in the sidebar, lists the receptionists that aren't archived, 10 to a page: the default first with a **Default** badge, then the others in the business's order. Each row shows the receptionist's page address (with **Not listed on the business page** under it when it isn't), its status, what it **Answers on** (its phone numbers, website widgets and QR links, or **Its page only**) and its language. From two receptionists on, a search box finds them by name or what they help with, and **Status** filters **All**, **Active**, **Paused** or **Archived**, each with its count. Everyone on the team reads the page; owners and admins change it.

- **Add receptionist** opens **Add a receptionist**: its name (required), what it helps with, its page address, its language, its knowledge, **Listed on the business page**, and **Copy settings from**, which gives it another receptionist's greeting, booking, transfer and instructions (the default one's unless another is picked). It starts active. The **Add receptionist** item at the bottom of every receptionist dropdown opens this page with `?add=1`, which opens the form.
- **Edit details** in a row's menu changes the same fields, and saves only what changed. **AI settings** opens `/agent?receptionist=<id>`.
- **Pause** keeps a receptionist and its settings while its numbers, widgets and QR links answer as the default receptionist; **Resume** brings it back. A paused receptionist doesn't count against the plan. Its visitors' open chats end: a visitor's next message on its page or widget starts a new chat with the default receptionist, and the old one stays closed in **Messages**, unless the team has taken it over, which then carries on. The maintainer chose this over letting the chat carry on with the default answering ([decisions](../project/decisions.md#choose-what-to-build)).
- **Archive** asks first. The receptionist leaves every list and dropdown, its entry points answer as the default receptionist, its visitors' open chats end as on a pause, and its calls, chats and questions stay, under its name (the **Questions** page adds "(archived)"). **Archived** in the status filter lists archived receptionists with the day each was archived, and **Restore** brings one back, paused if the plan's active receptionists are all in use.
- **Make default** asks first. The receptionist must be active. It takes over the business page and whatever names no receptionist, and the old default keeps its phone numbers, widgets and QR links and gets a page of its own. The new default gives up a language of its own and speaks the business's from then on, as AI settings shows it. It also answers from every article, since whatever falls back to the default relies on them, and **Edit details** can limit it afterwards. The old default keeps the knowledge it had. The business's shared settings don't move.
- **Order:** with three or more receptionists and no search or filter, each row has **Move up** and **Move down** buttons; the default is always first. **Sort A–Z** asks, then lists them by name from then on: while the order is alphabetical, a receptionist that's added, restored or renamed takes its place by name, and once someone moves one, the order is theirs and new ones go last. Names are compared as people read them in the business's language, accents and case aside and "Store 2" before "Store 10". The same order is used by every dropdown, the business page and the agent's list of the others.

The default receptionist can't be paused, archived or moved, and stays listed: make another one the default first. It has no language of its own either: `PATCH /api/receptionists/{receptionistId}` refuses one (409 `default_receptionist`), and **AI settings** changes the business's.

### Edit one receptionist's AI settings

With one receptionist, **AI settings** is as it was. With several, it has two parts. **Shared by every receptionist** holds the business summary, the AI and recording notice, the business's language and time zone, opening hours and the appointment change rules. **Each receptionist's own** starts with a **Receptionist** dropdown, which keeps its choice in the address (`?receptionist=<id>`), and holds that receptionist's greeting, language, transfer and booking. **Test Call** has a **Receptionist for test calls** dropdown, so an owner can hear each one.

## Know how many a plan includes

| Plan | Active receptionists, the default included |
| --- | --- |
| Free | 1 |
| Starter | 1 |
| Pro | 5 |
| Enterprise and any custom plan | Up to the deployment's cap, 50 unless set |

- **The plan counts active receptionists.** Paused and archived ones don't count, so an owner over the limit pauses one to add another. The numbers come from `limits.receptionists` in each brand's `billing.json`, which `pnpm billing apply` writes to `billing_plans.receptionists` ([billing](../billing/README.md#know-the-plans)). `GET /api/billing` and `GET /api/v1/billing/plans` return it as `included.receptionists`, `GET /api/v1/billing/catalog` as each plan's `receptionists`, and `pnpm lobbystack billing plans` shows it in a **receptionists** column. The plan screens show it right after the voice minutes: **What's included** on **Settings > Plan**, a promo code's offer (which says when its plan has more than its tier), the plan cards on onboarding's plan step and in the upgrade dialog, and a **Receptionists** row in the onboarding comparison. Enterprise's reads as custom.
- **The deployment caps receptionists that aren't archived**, whatever the plan, at `MAX_RECEPTIONISTS_PER_BUSINESS` on the admin and the worker: a whole number from 1 to 500, 50 when it's unset or anything else ([variables](../deployment/guide/appendix-a-variables.md)). A plan without a number, such as Enterprise or a self-hosted deployment's, gets the cap. Every custom plan gets the same one: giving one Enterprise customer 10 and another 30 needs a number on the plan, which migration `0134` refuses for custom plans, so it waits until a customer needs it ([roadmap](../roadmap/receptionists-next.md#know-the-smaller-items)).
- **The page says where the business stands,** "2 of 5 active receptionists on your plan", and offers only what would give it more (`upgrade` in `GET /api/receptionists`'s `allowance`, from `receptionistUpgrade` in `packages/shared/src/receptionists.ts`):
  - **A plan it can buy** (`plan`), when a higher self-serve plan includes more receptionists, as Pro does for Free and Starter. On a plan with one receptionist, a card, **A receptionist for each location, team or language**, offers **See plans** to owners and admins and tells others to ask one, and **Add receptionist** opens the plans too. With more than one, the line says to pause one or upgrade, beside **See plans**.
  - **Enterprise, through sales** (`sales`), when no self-serve plan has more, as on Pro: the line says to pause one or contact sales about Enterprise, beside **Contact sales**, which writes to the deployment's sales address with the upgrade dialog's Enterprise subject. **Add receptionist** and a refused resume say the same. Only a higher tier counts as an upgrade, so a promo plan on Pro's tier gets this too, even one that includes fewer receptionists than Pro ([decisions](../project/decisions.md#choose-what-to-build)).
  - **Nothing** (`null`) on a self-hosted deployment, on Enterprise, and wherever the plan's number already reaches the deployment's cap: no card and no button, only the count and, at the cap, that a business can keep that many. A deployment that keeps one receptionist a business shows "1 of 1" and that cap.
  - The API's refusal (`receptionist_limit`) says the same in English.
- **A downgrade pauses the newest extra receptionists,** never the default. Polar's reconcile of a plan or state change, and the end of a managed plan, queue the worker's `receptionists.enforceAllowance` job, which pauses the active receptionists past the new plan's number, newest first, and writes a `receptionist.paused` audit entry with the reason `plan_limit` for each. Nothing is deleted; their numbers, widgets and links answer as the default receptionist, and the owner chooses which to resume. The job runs on its own so nothing about receptionists can hold up a payment.
- **A plan that has had subscribers keeps its number or raises it.** `pnpm billing apply` refuses fewer receptionists on a promo or other extra plan once a business has taken it (its `frozen_at`), whether its own entry lowers them or its tier's lower number reaches it, as it refuses new terms there: the refusal is `plan_frozen`, and nothing is written. So the businesses on it keep what it gave them; write the old number into its entry, or put the lower one under a new code. More receptionists are always allowed. Standard plans never freeze, so Pro's number can still go down, which [billing](../billing/README.md#know-where-the-plans-live) advises against for a plan people pay for: the preview flags it as **lowers an allowance**, and a business left over the new number has its newest extra receptionists paused at the next subscription event Polar sends for it, such as its renewal (`diffBillingCatalog` in `packages/domain/src/server/billingCatalog.ts`).

## Choose who answers each number, widget and sign

A phone number, a widget key and a QR link each name the receptionist that answers it. When that receptionist is paused or archived, the default one answers instead, and the screens say so ("Sales is paused, so Front desk answers for now"). A new number or widget key names the default receptionist until the owner picks another.

- **Settings > Phone number:** **Answered by** under the business's number, and with several numbers, **Other numbers**, each with its own dropdown. A choice saves at once: "Calls and texts to +1 604 555 0188 go to Sales now." The receptionist then takes that number's calls and texts from the next one on.
- **Settings > Website widget:** **Receptionist** on each key: "Chats and calls from this widget go to the receptionist you pick." A new key names the default receptionist unless the form picks another.
- **Settings > QR code:** **Answered by** on each QR link ([use QR links](#use-qr-links)).

A paused receptionist can be chosen; an archived one can't. Each change writes an audit entry (`phone_number.receptionist_changed`, `widget_key.receptionist_changed`, `entry_link.updated`). The v1 API's number and widget key fields are unchanged; receptionists aren't in the API yet ([API gaps](../reference/api-gaps.md)).

## Give each receptionist its knowledge

The business keeps one knowledge library. Each article (a file, a website page or a text entry) is **Available to** **Everyone**, the default, or to **Chosen receptionists**.

- **A receptionist with Every article** uses them all, as the default receptionist does unless the owner limits it. **Articles for everyone, and the ones chosen for it** is the choice for the others, and the one a new receptionist gets.
- **An article chosen only for receptionists that are all archived, or for none,** is left to the default receptionist, never to everyone, so a store's private notes don't leak to the others.
- **On the Knowledge page,** with several receptionists, an **Available to** column shows **Everyone** or the names it's for ("Kitsilano, Gastown +3"). A receptionist filter shows exactly what that receptionist can use. Selecting rows, up to 500 at once, offers **Available to** to change them together: **Add** receptionists, **Remove** them or **Replace** who they're for, or make them available to everyone. Adding a file, a website or a text entry has the same **Available to** field.
- **Website rules** give a website's pages under a path, such as `/stores/kitsilano/`, to one receptionist, on this crawl and every later one. A path typed with letters such as `é` is saved encoded, as page addresses are, so `/café/` matches the café pages, and the list shows it as typed. **Website rules** on the Knowledge page lists them with how many pages each gave, adds and deletes them, and importing a website can add rules as it starts (**Page rules**). Deleting a rule gives its pages back to everyone, unless someone also chose receptionists for them or another rule, of this website or another, gives them to the same receptionist. A page a rule chose says **by rule**, and a choice made by hand stays when the rule goes. While a rule's receptionist is archived, a crawl leaves the rule out, so a page it finds stays for everyone until restoring the receptionist applies its rules.
- **The business summary is written from articles for everyone,** so a receptionist's private articles never reach the other receptionists' prompts.
- **Available to chooses what's relevant, not what's secret.** It keeps a store's notes out of the other stores' answers, but it isn't access control: the default receptionist answers from every article unless the owner limits it, a paused or archived receptionist's calls and chats go to the default, and anyone can open an unlisted receptionist at its page address and ask it. Keep text visitors must never hear out of the knowledge library.

Calls and chats search only the articles the answering receptionist may use (`searchKnowledgeEvidence` filters by the receptionist; `receptionistMayUse` in `packages/shared/src/receptionists.ts` is the same rule in code). The v1 API's `POST /knowledge/search` (fork PR #130) searches as the default receptionist, with its text entries, and counts only what it may use. The business's snapshot keeps up to 50 text entries and a digest of 40 documents for each receptionist, chosen from what it may use.

## Know what visitors see

- **The business page, `/profile/<slug>`,** is answered by the default receptionist. When the business lists more than one, a **You're talking to** dropdown under the business's name shows who answers and lists the others, the default first and then the business's order, each with what it helps with and a link to its own page. From 8 receptionists the list has a search box.
- **A receptionist's page, `/profile/<slug>/<handle>`,** is the business page answered by that receptionist, with its name in the page's title. It works whether or not the receptionist is listed; an unlisted one's page stays out of search results. It shows the newest number that rings its receptionist, or, for a receptionist with no number of its own, the business's main line, the newest that rings the default receptionist. A paused or archived receptionist's page, and the default one's handle, send visitors to the business page.
- **The chat and the browser call on a page** go to the page's receptionist. The page signs a token naming it, for the business and the page's widget key, which the widget sends when its session starts (`receptionistToken`), so a visitor can't reach another receptionist by editing a request. On any other website, the widget key's receptionist answers. The key's greeting, the chat's first bubble, is written for the key's receptionist, the default one when it names none, so a page another receptionist answers leaves it out. A visitor's chat belongs to the receptionist it started with: on another receptionist's page, the next message starts a chat with that one, unless the team has taken the chat over, which then stays the visitor's.
- **Settings > Public profile** has a **Receptionists on your page** card with several receptionists: which are **Listed**, and the **Order**, **Alphabetical** or **Custom** with move buttons. A paused receptionist stays off the page until it resumes. Changes save as they're made.

## Use QR links

A QR link is a short address, `/q/<code>`, that a printed sign carries. It opens one receptionist's page, or the business page for the default receptionist, and counts the sign's scans. **Settings > QR code** starts with **Your QR codes**:

- **The table** lists the business page's own address first, then each link, newest first, 10 to a page: its label, who answers (with a note when a paused or archived receptionist's link answers as the default), its address and its **Scans, 30 days** with a small line of the days. A search box appears once a business has more than 10 links, and with several receptionists a receptionist filter.
- **New link** asks for a label, such as "Front window", which only the team sees, and with several receptionists who answers it, starting with the receptionist the table is filtered to. The code is made when the link is created: 6 lowercase letters and digits, without the ones people mix up (i, l, o, 0 and 1), unique across the deployment. It never changes, so a printed sign keeps working whatever the link is pointed at later.
- **Each row's menu** edits the label and who answers, copies the address, and pauses or resumes the link. A paused link opens the business page. Links are never deleted; a business keeps up to 1,000.
- **The sign** below the table is for the business page or the link picked in its **Link** dropdown. It carries the link's address, opens with the receptionist's name ("Questions? Ask Sales."), and with a chat, "Scan to chat with Sales". **Open** shows the page the link opens. A download is named for the business, the link's label and its code.
- **Scans per day** under a link's sign charts its last 30 days, with the total and the busiest day, and a table of the days for screen readers. The business page's own sign isn't counted: a link is needed to count scans.

`GET /q/<code>` counts the scan and redirects (307, never cached) to the page; `HEAD` redirects without counting, so a link preview isn't a scan. Every `GET` counts, with no limit per address, so a link's scans count opens, not people, and a script could raise them; nothing is billed or limited by scans. The maintainer chose this over counting a set number per address and hour ([decisions](../project/decisions.md#choose-what-to-build)). Scans are counted per link and UTC day, with nothing about the visitor, and days older than 13 months are deleted by the hourly privacy sweep, even with content retention off (`CONTENT_RETENTION_ENABLED`). A code that opens nothing answers 404 with "This QR code doesn't open a page" in the visitor's language. So does every link while the business page isn't published, and the scan isn't counted, since each page a link opens answers 404 until then, as **Settings > QR code** warns. Everyone on the team sees the links and copies addresses; owners and admins make and change them.

## Know who answered

- **Calls, conversations and follow-ups** name the receptionist that answered them. With several receptionists, **Calls**, **Messages** and **Questions** have a receptionist filter, kept in the address as `?receptionist=<id>`; the **Calls** table and a call's page show the call's **Receptionist**, and a conversation in **Messages** says "Receptionist: Sales". A text conversation names the receptionist of the number texted; when the business released a number and bought it again, the row in use names it, never the released one's. Every record names its receptionist from the moment it's written, so the filter shows exactly the records that receptionist answered.
- **Questions** shows who heard each question ("Heard by Kitsilano, Gastown"). Answering one asks **Who uses this answer**: **Every receptionist**, or **Only the receptionist that heard it** (or the ones that did), which saves the text entry for chosen receptionists.
- **What the receptionist says about the others:** when a business has several, each receptionist's prompt says who it is and lists the others that are listed and active, with what each helps with, up to 12 in the business's order, and how many more the business page lists. Nobody is handed over: in a chat the receptionist names the right one and gives its page's link, and on a call it names it and offers to take a message. Chats give these links only while the business page is published, and publishing or unpublishing it refreshes the snapshot. Handing a call or chat to another receptionist is on the [roadmap](../roadmap/receptionists-next.md).
- **Recorded greetings:** each active receptionist gets its own recorded opening, in its own language, whether or not it answers a phone number (since fork PR #139; before, only those answering a number did). Adding a receptionist, changing its greeting or language, or giving it a number queues a snapshot refresh, after which the worker records that receptionist's opening, so its next call opens with it. The dashboard's **Test Call** asks for the chosen receptionist's recording as the dashboard opens and when the choice changes, and waits up to 15 seconds for one still being made ([calls](calls.md#open-calls-with-a-recorded-greeting)). A business gets at most 100 new recordings a day, twice the most receptionists it can have, and its calls open with the greeting command past that ([voice runtime](../voice/runtime.md#open-calls-with-a-recording)).

## Know how it's built

- **Tables.** `receptionist_profiles` keeps one row per receptionist (migration `0133`): today's row became its business's default (`is_default`, one per business), with `name`, `description`, `handle`, `knowledge_scope`, `listed`, `sort_order`, `locale`, `status` and `archived_at`. The summary, the recording notice and the appointment change rules moved to `businesses`, and `0143` dropped the default receptionist's copies of them. `billing_plans.receptionists` (`0134`). `knowledge_document_receptionists`, `knowledge_snippet_receptionists` and `knowledge_website_rules` (`0136`, with `0135`'s key for their composite foreign keys). `receptionist_id` on `phone_numbers`, `widget_keys`, `calls`, `conversations` and `inbox_items`, and `unanswered_question_receptionists` (`0137`). `entry_links` and `entry_link_daily_scans` (`0140`). `0143` makes `receptionist_id` required on `calls`, `conversations` and `inbox_items`. Every new table has forced row-level security, and links name their business with composite foreign keys, so the database refuses a link between two businesses.
- **Who answers, in the database.** `app.effective_receptionist` gives the receptionist an entry point names when it may answer, else the default; `app.resolve_receptionist_by_phone` and `app.resolve_entry_link` find them for a call and a scan before a request knows its business. A trigger gives every new business its default receptionist, named in the business's language (`app.add_default_receptionist`, `0143`), and another gives every new call, conversation, follow-up, number and widget key the default receptionist when its writer names none. So the snapshot, AI settings and the v1 API always find a default receptionist, and never stand in for a missing one.
- **The snapshot.** The business's snapshot carries every receptionist that isn't archived, and `snapshotForReceptionist` (`packages/domain/src/snapshot.ts`) cuts one receptionist's view from it: its own settings, the articles it may use, and the others for its prompt. Any change to receptionists refreshes the snapshot. The rebuild ranks each receptionist's documents and text entries on two partial indexes (`0141` and `0142`): on a test business with 5,000 website pages, 50 receptionists ranked in about 30 ms instead of about a second. The rebuild and the knowledge search turn PostgreSQL's just-in-time compiling off (`SET LOCAL jit = off`), which took longer than the queries it compiled.
- **What keeps it quick with many receptionists.** Each admin and worker process keeps the snapshots it parsed in memory (`redisSnapshotCache` in `packages/domain/src/server/snapshotCache.ts`). While Redis names the same version under `widget:snapshot-version:<business>`, a read fetches only that version, so a refresh still shows as soon as Redis has it: a read at 50 receptionists takes about 1 ms instead of 22 for fetching and parsing 2.3 MB. A kept snapshot is frozen, since every reader shares it; the ones kept take up to 16 MB of JSON, least recently read go first, and each is parsed again after a minute. The job dispatcher queues one rebuild for all of a business's `snapshot.refresh` rows in a batch and marks the others published (`lobbystack.outbox.coalesced` counts them), so a save that queues two or three, or a crawl's pages, rebuilds once. Adding a website rule reads only the site's pages under its paths, filtered in the database (`websiteRulePagePrefixes`): about 2 ms instead of 20 to 45 ms for 5,000 pages, which were then matched in code. A new order, **Sort A–Z** or a change to the listed receptionists is one statement instead of one per receptionist, and adding, restoring or renaming one reads the list once.
- **Domain:** `packages/domain/src/server/receptionists.ts` (the page, the plan allowance, adding, pausing, archiving, the default and the order), `receptionistInput.ts` (the checks of what the Receptionists page sends, with the messages and codes it shows), `receptionistProfile.ts` (AI settings for one receptionist), `receptionistEntryPoints.ts` (numbers and widget keys), `receptionistRecords.ts` (the receptionist a record names, and the ones answering calls), `knowledgeAudience.ts` (audiences and website rules), `entryLinks.ts` (QR links and scans) and `publicProfiles.ts` (the pages). They share `requestHelpers.ts` with the public API: the error the admin routes answer with its status and code, the check that a body is an object, and the check for a unique index's refusal, which reads the whole chain of causes. The shared rules are in `packages/shared/src/receptionists.ts`.
- **Agent:** `receptionistGuidance` in `packages/agent-core/src/instructions.ts` writes the lines about the others; the tools search and save as the answering receptionist (`packages/agent-core/src/tools.ts`).
- **Routes:** `/api/receptionists` and the routes under it, `/api/entry-links`, `/api/knowledge/audience`, `/api/knowledge/website-rules`, `PATCH /api/phone-numbers/{phoneNumberId}`, `/q/{code}` and `/profile/{slug}/{handle}`, and changes to AI settings, the widget keys, the widget's session and the lists ([route catalog](../reference/routes.md)).
- **Dashboard:** the page renders `LiveReceptionistsSurface` (`apps/admin/src/components/live-receptionists-surface.tsx`); the shared dropdown, multiselect box and filter are in `apps/admin/src/components/receptionists/`, and the QR links in `apps/admin/src/components/qr-code/`. **AI settings** (`live-agent-basic-settings-surface.tsx`) loads the settings, saves each change and lays out its two parts; each setting is a component of its own in `apps/admin/src/components/agent-settings/`, the shared ones in `business-settings.tsx` and each receptionist's own in `receptionist-settings.tsx`, mounted afresh for each receptionist. Words are in the `receptionists` namespace and the `agent`, `settings`, `knowledge` and `profile` ones, in English, French, Spanish and Serbian.
- **Tests:** the shared rules (`packages/shared/src/receptionists.test.ts`), integration tests against PostgreSQL for receptionists, entry points, records and what migration `0143` guarantees, audiences and website rules, and QR links (`packages/domain/src/server/*.integration.test.ts`), the billing catalog's refusal of fewer receptionists (`billingCatalog.test.ts` and `billingCatalog.integration.test.ts`), the snapshot views, the agent's lines and tools, every new route, and rendering tests for each screen, among them `live-receptionists-table.test.tsx` and `live-qr-code-links.test.tsx`. Since the second review (fork PR #135): a plan change from Polar to the worker's pauses, end to end, with the newest paused first and the default kept; two adds racing for a plan's last place on committed rows, of which one gets through; restoring at the cap; every message and code of the input checks and every wording of the plan-limit refusal (`receptionistInput.test.ts`, `receptionists.test.ts`, `entryLinks.test.ts`); the worker's `receptionists.enforceAllowance` job (`apps/worker/src/handlers.test.ts`); and route tests whose domain mocks are typed and whose rows come from builders that fill every field (`apps/admin/src/lib/receptionist-testing.ts`), so a field the domain renames fails the typecheck.
- **Browser journeys:** `apps/admin/e2e/receptionists.e2e.ts`, in CI's list in `scripts/e2e-local.sh`. In the first, an owner on the free plan sees the upgrade card and the plans behind **Add receptionist**, where the Pro card offers 5 receptionists, then on Pro adds two receptionists, gives one its own greeting on **AI settings**, archives one only after confirming, and restores it. In the second, a visitor on the business page picks a listed receptionist under **You're talking to**, whose chat answers as it through the page's signed token; an unlisted receptionist's page opens too; and a QR link made on **Settings > QR code** opens its receptionist's page, counts a scan but not a link preview's `HEAD`, passes no referrer on, and opens the business page while its receptionist is paused.

## Deploy it

Fork PR #129 stays off `main` until the new release it's for, so the deployments that follow `main` and `alloplace/r1` keep what they run ([decisions](../project/decisions.md#choose-what-to-build)). Several receptionists are for new deployments: migrations `0133` to `0143` run with the ones before them on an empty database, as any new deployment's do ([decisions](../project/decisions.md#choose-what-to-build)). So the clean-up the design planned for a week after shipping is `0143` itself, in the same release: every business gets its default receptionist with it, calls, conversations and follow-ups always name their receptionist, the settings every receptionist shares live only on `businesses`, and there is no backfill to wait for. Nothing needs to be set: `MAX_RECEPTIONISTS_PER_BUSINESS` is optional.

- **The billing file.** Both brands' `billing.json` have `limits.receptionists`, so after the deploy, apply the file as after any billing change (`pnpm billing apply`, [billing](../billing/README.md#sign-in-preview-apply-and-check)). Migration `0134` already wrote the same numbers to the plans, and Polar never sees them, so the apply changes no plan and no Polar product, and the plans' numbers hold meanwhile. Until it runs, the daily `billing.verifyCatalog` check reports the newer file as drift.
- **A deployment that already has businesses** can run the same migrations, but they aren't built for it: `0143` gives each business without one its default receptionist and names it on every older call, conversation and follow-up, in one transaction that gives up after 5 seconds waiting for a lock, and nothing spares a large table its locks. The release before can't run beside it, since `0140` and `0143` drop an index and columns it reads, so the dashboard's AI settings and the worker's snapshots fail until the new admin and worker serve. Take a copy of the database first: there's no rolling back to a release before receptionists.

## Know what was decided while building it

The maintainer's nine answers, the four given after the code review and the two after the second review are on the [decisions](../project/decisions.md#choose-what-to-build) page. These smaller choices were made while building it:

- **Every entry point names its receptionist.** A new number or widget key gets the default receptionist of the moment unless the owner picks another, so making another receptionist the default leaves each with the one it had.
- **Phone numbers' Answered by and the business page's card save as soon as they change**, like the toggles beside them; the forms with several fields save with a button.
- **The order is kept as positions, and "alphabetical" is worked out** from the names rather than stored as a setting: while the positions are in name order, a receptionist added, restored or renamed takes its place by name. **Sort A–Z** writes name order. Moving a receptionist uses buttons, not dragging, which works with a keyboard and a screen reader.
- **The business page lists receptionists through one setting**, `PUT /api/receptionists/listed`, so the card saves the whole choice in one step.
- **QR links:** **Open** goes straight to the receptionist's page, so the owner's check isn't counted as a scan. The sign's words name the link's receptionist, and the default receptionist's link keeps the general words. The message typed for a sign resets when another link is picked. Downloads are named for the business, the link's label and its code. The list carries each link's last 30 days, so the table's lines and the chart need no second request. A label is required on the dashboard, though `POST /api/entry-links` accepts a link without one, which the table calls "Link <code>". Viewers can copy addresses.
- **The dropdowns are the dashboard's own**, not a library's, with a search box from 8 receptionists and the **Add receptionist** and **Manage receptionists** items at the bottom.
- **French and Spanish** call a receptionist *la réceptionniste* and *la recepcionista*, and the default receptionist's name is translated in 14 languages (`DEFAULT_RECEPTIONIST_NAMES`). Changing the business's language renames the default receptionist only while it has the old language's name, the one the code gave it: a name someone wrote stays, and so does another language's name, which only someone could have chosen. The rename is in the audit log as `receptionist.updated`, with the reason `business_language`, the old and new names and who changed the language, as a rename on the Receptionists page is.
