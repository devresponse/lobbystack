# Choose the brand name

On 2026-10-10, right after the [QR place guide](qr-place-guide-market.md) study, three screens looked for a brand for the guide's signs: names built on "Ask" (60 candidates), call-to-action names beyond "Ask" (72 names and 6 domain hacks), and Allo- and Hello- combinations (37), plus bare "Allo" with other domain endings. Each checked whether the .com, .ai and .io (and for some, .ca) were registered, whether a company or app already used the name, and whether US or Canadian trademark filings stood in the way. These were knockout screens on public registers, not legal clearance, and the research registered, bought and filed nothing. **AlloPlace came out on top and is the brand the project now ships** (`brands/alloplace/`, [branding](../branding.md)). Domains and filings change weekly, so re-check before acting on any of them.

## Know why AlloPlace

- **Short, bilingual and on concept:** "Allô" is how French speakers answer the phone, and "place" is a public square in French as well as a place in English.
- **Clear on the day:** alloplace.com, .ai, .io and .ca were all unregistered, with no US or Canadian filings and no users found, at about $213 upfront for all four at list prices.
- **The shared risk:** every Allo- name sits near ALLO Fiber's live US ALLO marks for communication services, and the UK's Hello Lamp Post already sells talking places under "Hello" (no US registration found). Write the name as one word; bare "Allo" collides with those marks, and allo.com, allo.ai and allo.ca are taken.
- **Before filing:** a trademark attorney's clearance search in both countries, then register the domains and file on the same day, since on-concept names kept being claimed within weeks. A .ca needs a Canadian presence.

## Follow the sign principles

- **Brand the greeting, print the instruction.** A greeting invites without instructing and crosses the language line: "Hey, [Place]!" becomes "Allô, [Lieu]!". The instruction ("Scan to ask", "Tap to talk") is sign copy anyone may print, not the brand: everyday phrases such as ShowMeAround, TalkHere, WhatsHere and SayHiHere fail as marks, because examiners refuse them and apps already trade under them.
- **The place owner keeps the headline.** "Ask Union Station" or "Allô, [Lieu]!" in large type, with a small "by AlloPlace" endorsement. A place-noun brand can contradict the headline; a greeting never does.
- **Plan for Quebec.** Under rules in force since 2025-06-01, an English mark may stand untranslated on a sign only if it is registered in Canada (a pending application doesn't count), and French text gets at least twice the mark's space on signs visible from outside, including inside malls. Register the mark in Canada before launching there.
- **Keep "AI" off the signs,** and print a short, spellable web address under every code, since the FTC warned of fake QR stickers in September 2026. Show where to tap with the NFC mark.
- **Pair any "Hey" name with "Tap to talk".** "Hey" is a smart speaker's wake word and can suggest an always-listening device.

## Compare the shortlists

| Screen | Top names, in order | What decided it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Ask" plus a word | AskThisPlace, AskQuarter, AskCorner, AskBearings, AskCommons, AskPlaza, AskVillage, AskGuide, AskTheWalls, AskDistrict | "Ask" is a weak, crowded prefix: IAC still holds the ASK marks behind Ask.com and has beaten ASK LOCALS and ASKAROUND, so protection rests on the second word. AskThisPlace led only if its .com, registered by an unknown holder on 2026-07-26, could be had. AskLocal, AskNearby, AskPlace, AskHere, AskAround, AskMap and AskCompass were taken or blocked, and Ask Mona already sells QR museum guides |
| Call-to-action names | HeyThisPlace, HeyQuarter, AlloVille, HeyCommons, TalkToThisPlace, HeyHere, HeyPlaza, HeyLandmark | Only 9 of 72 .coms were free. HEY is shared ground (37signals' HEY marks) and HERE is crowded. AlloVille was the one finalist greeting native to French, so outside Quebec's rules for English marks |
| Allo- and Hello- | **AlloPlace**, AlloPlaza, HelloQuarter, AlloDistrict | All four domains free for each, no filings, no users found |
| Bare "Allo" with other endings | allo.place, allo.guide, allo.town and allo.zone were free | Trademark offices ignore the ending, so allo.anything is just ALLO. Advised: "Allô" as the sign's greeting and allo.place at most as a printed short link |

Two names asked about along the way were advised against: Scana (its .com, .ai and .io taken, and live SCANA ENERGY marks) and HelloZone (.com priced at $25,000 and HELLO crowded). ScanToAsk was already a live sign-up page, so "Scan to ask" works only as sign copy.

## Test the sound before committing

None of the screens tested how the names sound. A hear-it-once spelling test with 10 to 20 English speakers and a francophone panel in Montreal and Quebec City would settle it; in Quebec, the guide should introduce itself in French by the place's name rather than by an English brand.
